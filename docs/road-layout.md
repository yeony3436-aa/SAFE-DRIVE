# Extended intersection road layout

This is a focused visual simulation scene, not a certified road design. Coordinates
are in meters. The ego drives east (`+x`) in the south lane (`y=-1.75`); the
configured pedestrian crosses north (`+y`) at `x=-5`.

| Marking or fixture | Geometry in the simulation |
| --- | --- |
| East-west road | `x=-240..150`, `y=-4..4`; ego spawn defaults to `x=-210` |
| North-south arms | `x=-2..6`, from `y=4..22` and `y=-42..-4` |
| West crosswalk | Eight white bars, each 3.6 m along `x` and 0.5 m along `y`; bar centers at `x=-5`, `y=-3.45..3.55` |
| Eastbound stop line | `x=-9.25`, 0.30 m along `x`, 3.30 m across the lane |
| Westbound stop line in intersection world | `x=8.4`, before the east edge of the central square |
| Vehicle signal | Three circular aspects in a horizontal roadside housing facing the eastbound approach |
| Pedestrian signal | Two square aspects in a vertical curbside housing at the south crossing entry |

The nearest west crosswalk edge is `x=-6.8`. The eastbound stop-line edge is
`x=-9.10`, leaving 2.30 m of bare road between them. On red, the controller
uses a configured 1.30 m front-bumper target margin; validation requires at
least 0.60 m of measured clearance. The crossing
bars are parallel to vehicle travel and repeat across the walking direction;
the prior bars were rotated 90 degrees. Paint has no collision geometry.

The geometry follows the visual pattern in the [Korean Road Traffic Act
Enforcement Rule, Annex 6](https://www.law.go.kr/flDownload.do?flNm=%5B%EB%B3%84%ED%91%9C+6%5D+%EC%95%88%EC%A0%84%ED%91%9C%EC%A7%80%EC%9D%98+%EC%A2%85%EB%A5%98%2C+%EB%A7%8C%EB%93%9C%EB%8A%94+%EB%B0%A9%EC%8B%9D+%EB%B0%8F+%EC%84%A4%EC%B9%98%E3%86%8D%EA%B4%80%EB%A6%AC%EA%B8%B0%EC%A4%80%28%EC%A0%9C8%EC%A1%B0%EC%A0%9C2%ED%95%AD+%EB%B0%8F+%EC%A0%9C11%EC%A1%B0%EC%A0%9C1%ED%98%B8+%EA%B4%80%EB%A0%A8%29&flSeq=95408325)
for crossing marking 532 and stop-line marking 530. The [FHWA MUTCD 11th
Edition, Part 3, Sections 3B.19 and 3C.03–3C.07](https://mutcd.fhwa.dot.gov/pdfs/11th_Edition/part3.pdf)
also illustrates high-visibility bars parallel to approaching traffic and a
stop line in advance of the crossing. These sources informed the drawing;
the dimensions above are project configuration, not a claim of regulatory
approval in a particular jurisdiction.

## Steering validation courses

The same intersection world has three eastbound routes: fast straight to `(130, -1.75)`,
right to `(0.25, -30)`, and left to `(3.75, 16)`. The right-turn path uses a
4.6 m centreline radius and the left-turn path uses a 5.5 m radius. The current
signal controls the eastbound approach for all three routes; other approaches
do not yet have independent signal control.

The long west arm permits acceleration and braking before the same intersection.
The fast straight route reached 17.07 m/s in one headless run but exceeded the
1.75 m chassis-corner corridor criterion. The turn routes use a lower approach
speed and slow to 0.8 m/s for the compact corner; both passed their route
criteria in stored runs. These are simulator speeds, not real-road ratings.

The south arm now extends 12 m beyond the right-turn destination. Its asphalt,
lane center/edge markings, sidewalks, grass, and visual curbs continue to the
end. The southbound curbs begin beyond the turn corner at `y=-7`, leaving the
turning footprint clear in the displayed scene. Curbs and sidewalks are visual
only; wheel contact comes from the level road collision surface. A vehicle that
entered the eastbound stop line on green keeps that entry permission through
the turn if the light changes.

`curve_course.world` is a separate road geometry test. An 8 m-wide road begins
west of `x=-10`, turns north on a 12 m road-centre radius, and continues to
`y=40`. Its right-hand lane route has an approximately 13.75 m radius and ends
at `(3.75, 32)`. The road surface uses short, overlapping static collision and
visual boxes generated by `safe_drive_sim/scripts/generate_curve_course.py`.
The bend is a test course, not yet joined to the signalized intersection.

## Connected road grid

`connected_road.world` is generated from the validated signal world by
`safe_drive_sim/scripts/generate_connected_road.py`. It retains the central
crosswalk, stop line, vehicle and pedestrian lamps, and their ROS-controlled
positions. The original world and its longer test routes remain separate.

The new world has three east-west road centerlines at `y=-56, 0, 56 m` and
three north-south centerlines at `x=-36, 2, 36 m`. Each road is 8 m wide and
has matching asphalt visual and collision boxes. The horizontal surfaces run
from `x=-40` to `40 m`; the vertical surfaces run from `y=-60` to `60 m`.
The central north-south road is extended to both outer roads. Thus the nine
junctions form a connected 3×3 grid with no terminal road branch. Junction
centerlines have paint gaps; outer junctions are currently unsignalized.
Grass and pavements are visual only, so they do not create hidden barriers.

```text
(-36, 56) ───── (2, 56) ───── (36, 56)
    │               │               │
(-36,  0) ──── (2,  0) ───── (36,  0)
    │            signal             │
(-36,-56) ───── (2,-56) ───── (36,-56)
```

`connected_left_loop.yaml` and `connected_straight_loop.yaml` start and return
at `(-30, -1.75) m`. The revised `connected_right_loop.yaml` starts and
returns near `(-27, -1.75) m`, leaving a straight segment after its last bend
for the chassis to align. Each takes its named movement at the central signal
and returns along grid roads. The route generator is
`safe_drive_control/scripts/generate_connected_routes.py`. The eastbound
central signal still controls the first approach. The current follower has a
single eastbound stop-line rule, so it does not govern the other eight
junctions. This is a connected map and a set of configured routes, not yet a
general intersection-aware planner.

Headless Gazebo validation on 2026-11-03 reached the start again on all three
routes. Left and straight passed the configured chassis-corner corridor check.
The original right route reached its start but exceeded the `1.75 m` corridor
by `0.046 m` at the lower west junction. The revised route passed at configured
1.2 and 1.6 m/s after path-heading correction and curve-aware speed limiting.
The central fixed-RED stop passed with `0.918 m` minimum front-bumper
clearance. Results are in `docs/validation/`.

The connected-world generator also writes `config/connected_road_markings.json`
from the same painted intervals used in the world. A separate odometry monitor
checks the four chassis corners against centerline and edge-line footprints on
those straight intervals. Junction gaps without paint are excluded. The first
four-case run found right-turn edge-line overlap even though its route corridor
passed, so the two measurements must be interpreted separately.

The east-side junction has a separate unsignalized pedestrian crossing at
`x=27 m` and an eastbound yield line at `x=22.75 m`. Eight crossing bars span
the 8 m east-west road; the nearest bar edge and yield-line edge are 2.30 m
apart. The eastbound vehicle turns right into the southbound lane centered at
`x=34.25 m`. There is no vehicle signal at this junction. The painted marks
have no collision geometry; the road surface does. The pedestrian starts at
`(27,-4.25) m` and crosses to `y=5.2 m`.
